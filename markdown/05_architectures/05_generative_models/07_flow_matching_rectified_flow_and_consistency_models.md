# Flow Matching, Rectified Flow, and Consistency Models

## Executive Summary & ELI5

Traditional diffusion models formulate continuous-time generative modeling by adding Gaussian noise via stochastic differential equations (SDEs) and learning score functions to reverse the diffusion trajectory. However, curved SDE paths require dozens to hundreds of evaluation steps during sampling, which imposes significant computational overhead.

**Flow Matching (FM)**, **Rectified Flow (RF)**, and **Consistency Models (CM)** revolutionize this paradigm by replacing curved diffusion paths with straight probability flows and direct boundary-to-origin mapping:
- **Flow Matching**: Directly regresses the velocity field of a continuous-time probability density path. Conditional Flow Matching (CFM) constructs linear interpolation trajectories between simple source noise $x_0 \sim p_0$ and complex target data $x_1 \sim p_1$, admitting a simple closed-form constant velocity target $u_t = x_1 - x_0$.
- **Rectified Flow**: Formulates generative modeling as ordinary differential equations (ODEs) connecting arbitrary distributions. Through an iterative "reflow" procedure, trajectories are progressively straightened, minimizing transport cost and enabling high-fidelity 1-step or 2-step sampling.
- **Consistency Models**: Learn a function that maps any intermediate point $x_t$ along a continuous probability trajectory directly back to its trajectory origin $x_\epsilon$. By enforcing self-consistency between points on the same ODE trajectory, consistency models allow high-fidelity generation in a single neural network forward pass without adversarial training.

## Mathematical Foundations & Governing Equations

### 1. Continuous Normalizing Flows and Continuity Equation
Let $p_t(x)$ be a probability density path for $t \in [0, 1]$ generated by a vector field $v_t(x)$. The time evolution of $p_t$ satisfies the continuity equation:
$$\frac{\partial p_t(x)}{\partial t} + \nabla \cdot (p_t(x) v_t(x)) = 0$$

### 2. Conditional Flow Matching (CFM)
Marginal flow matching optimizes the expected squared difference against the intractable marginal vector field $u_t(x)$. Lipman et al. established that matching conditional vector fields $u_t(x | x_1)$ induces the exact marginal flow:
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{CFM}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E}_{t \sim \mathcal{U}[0, 1], x_0 \sim p_0, x_1 \sim p_1} \left[ \| v_\theta(x_t, t) - u_t(x_t | x_0, x_1) \|^2 \right]$$

For straight-line Optimal Transport Conditional Flow Matching (OT-CFM):
$$x_t = (1 - t) x_0 + t x_1, \quad u_t(x_t | x_0, x_1) = \frac{d x_t}{dt} = x_1 - x_0$$
The neural velocity network $v_\theta(x_t, t)$ simply learns to predict the constant displacement vector $x_1 - x_0$.

### 3. Rectified Flow and the Reflow Procedure
Given empirical pairs $(X_0, X_1)$, 1-Rectified Flow learns the velocity $v(x, t)$ driving $X_0 \to X_1$. To straighten curved ODE trajectories, the **Reflow** procedure generates synthetic endpoint pairs $(Z_0, Z_1 = \text{ODE\_Solve}(Z_0))$ by integrating the learned vector field, and retrains $v$:
$$\mathcal{L}_{\text{reflow}}(\theta) = \mathbb{E} \left[ \| v_\theta(t Z_1 + (1-t) Z_0, t) - (Z_1 - Z_0) \|^2 \right]$$
Repeated reflow steps asymptotically straighten paths, reducing truncation error for coarse Euler steps $x_{t+\Delta t} = x_t + \Delta t \cdot v(x_t, t)$.

### 4. Consistency Models and Parameterization
Consistency models define a function $f_\theta(x, t)$ that maps any trajectory point to the terminal state $x_\epsilon$:
$$f_\theta(x_t, t) = x_\epsilon, \quad \forall t \in [\epsilon, T]$$
The boundary condition $f_\theta(x, \epsilon) = x$ is guaranteed architecturally via skip connections:
$$f_\theta(x, t) = c_{\text{skip}}(t) x + c_{\text{out}}(t) F_\theta(x, t)$$
where:
$$c_{\text{skip}}(t) = \frac{\sigma_{\text{data}}^2}{(t - \epsilon)^2 + \sigma_{\text{data}}^2}, \quad c_{\text{out}}(t) = \frac{\sigma_{\text{data}}(t - \epsilon)}{\sqrt{\sigma_{\text{data}}^2 + t^2}}$$
At $t = \epsilon$, $c_{\text{skip}}(\epsilon) = 1$ and $c_{\text{out}}(\epsilon) = 0$, guaranteeing the identity map by construction.

## Tensor Memory Layout & Implementation Details

### Velocity Network Architecture
The baseline velocity network $v_\theta(x, t)$ processes the state tensor $x \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times D}$ and scalar time $t \in \mathbb{R}^{B \times 1}$:
1. **Time Embedding**:
   $$t_{\text{emb}} = \text{SiLU}(t \cdot W_t + b_t), \quad W_t \in \mathbb{R}^{1 \times D_h}, b_t \in \mathbb{R}^{D_h}$$
2. **State Projection & Modulation**:
   $$h = \text{SiLU}(x \cdot W_x + b_x + t_{\text{emb}}), \quad W_x \in \mathbb{R}^{D \times D_h}, b_x \in \mathbb{R}^{D_h}$$
3. **Output Velocity Projection**:
   $$v_{\text{pred}} = h \cdot W_{\text{out}} + b_{\text{out}}, \quad W_{\text{out}} \in \mathbb{R}^{D_h \times D}, b_{\text{out}} \in \mathbb{R}^D$$

### Numerical Euler Integration
Inference generation progresses from $t=0$ to $t=1$:
$$x_{k+1} = x_k + \Delta t \cdot v_\theta(x_k, t_k)$$
With straightened flows, $\Delta t = 1.0$ (single step) yields high visual and physical fidelity.

## Pathological Misuse & Failure Modes

1. **Failure to Enforce Boundary Conditions in Consistency Models**:
   - *Anti-Pattern*: Training an unconstrained neural network $f_\theta(x, t)$ with penalization losses to satisfy $f_\theta(x, \epsilon) \approx x$.
   - *Consequence*: Accumulated error at $t \to \epsilon$ causes severe mode collapse and blurred reconstructions.
   - *Remedy*: Always enforce boundary conditions strictly via analytical skip-connections ($c_{\text{skip}}, c_{\text{out}}$).

2. **Trajectory Crossing in Non-Rectified Straight Flows**:
   - *Anti-Pattern*: Sampling independent pairs $(x_0, x_1)$ from uncoupled marginals without Optimal Transport or reflow straightening.
   - *Consequence*: Trajectories intersect in Euclidean space, causing ambiguous velocity fields where $v_\theta(x, t)$ averages contradictory flow directions, leading to phantom artifacts and ghosting.
   - *Remedy*: Use OT-CFM mini-batch optimal transport matching or execute 2-Rectified Flow (reflow) to disentangle trajectory intersections.

3. **Inappropriate Step-Size in Curved Flow Regions**:
   - *Anti-Pattern*: Using fixed large Euler steps ($\Delta t = 0.5$) on un-rectified models near the boundary $t \approx 0$ or $t \approx 1$.
   - *Consequence*: Severe numerical truncation drift that escapes the data manifold.
   - *Remedy*: Use adaptive ODE solvers (e.g. Dormand-Prince / RK45) or perform consistency distillation.
